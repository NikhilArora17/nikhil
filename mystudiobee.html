Numbers You Can Trust

Every total on screen has to be traceable to a line item. No rounding surprises, no totals that don't match the PDF the client receives.

Principle 02

The person designing this is also the person using it at 11pm after a shoot. Every extra click is a tax on the exact same person twice over.

Unlike the public site, this tool is opened at a desk between shoots, not on a commute. Density and keyboard speed beat mobile polish here.

Information Architecture

IA & Nav Structure

A sidebar, not a scroll —
grouped by how often you visit.

There's no landing page to design for here — just a dashboard and a permanent sidebar. So the real IA work was grouping: daily workflow at the top, document lifecycle in the middle, configuration and safety nets pushed to the edges.

Dashboard

Admin · Team, Services, Cost Model, Profit Split, Equipment, Vendors, Hires
Insights · Reports, Time Log
Bin — owner/admin only, kept separate

Why Billing Is One Group

One editor, four documents

Quote → proforma → invoice → receipt is a linear lifecycle. All four share one editor component, so grouping them under a single nav label matches how the code — and the money — actually flows.

Why Admin Is Pushed Down

Configuration, not daily use

Team, cost model, profit split, equipment, vendor and hire ratings are set-and-forget. Keeping them out of the primary flow means the daily-use items never compete for attention.

Why Bin Sits Alone

A safety net, not a workflow

Deliberately separated from every other group, owner/admin only, and visually distinct — you should only ever land here on purpose, after a mistake, not while browsing.

Primary Path

Dashboard → Quote → Invoice

The dashboard's "+ Quote" shortcut and Recent Quotes list exist because that's the single most common path into the app — money starting to move.

Designing for Constraints · 01

Line-Item Views

One document, three levels
of how much detail to show.

Full itemised breakdowns were right for some clients and unnecessarily granular for others — but rebuilding the same quote twice wasn't an option. The fix was a per-document toggle, not a global setting: Itemised, Grouped, and Summary, switchable on the same line items.

Grouped View Iteration

Grouped view first shipped with its own editable "Total shown on PDF" override row — a second source of truth for the total. It was removed once the real Subtotal/GST/Discount/Total box below already covered it; one number, one place it lives. Summary and Grouped overrides also default to the pre-GST amount, not the GST-inclusive total, since that read as confusing tax-inclusive pricing at a glance.

Designing for Constraints · 02

Internal Costing

Purchase price isn't cost —
a subscription bills every year.

Equipment inventory only ever tracked one-time purchase cost and rental rates — useful for quoting gear to a client, useless for knowing what running the studio actually costs per month. A dedicated Internal Costing tab followed, and even that needed one more refinement: a billing-period field, so an annual Adobe subscription converts to the right monthly figure instead of a manually-guessed one.

Billing Period Refinement

Shipped first as a flat "amount → monthly cost" split. That silently assumed everything recurring was billed monthly, which wasn't true — Adobe bills annually. Adding a Billed field (Monthly / Annual) with an auto-calculated, still-editable monthly figure meant the P&L report's overhead number finally matched what the studio was actually paying.

Recurring vs. One-Time

Two costing types, one table: recurring subscriptions and one-time purchases amortised over their useful life, both collapsing to a single "effective monthly cost" column.

Auto-Calculated, Editable

The monthly figure is computed from amount and billing period by default, but stays a plain editable field — the system's best guess, not a locked value.

Feeds the P&L Directly

Active internal costing items sum straight into the P&L report's overhead line, so margin reflects real running cost, not just per-project labor and direct spend.

Designing for Constraints · 03

Client Bin

Delete had to stop meaning
“gone forever, instantly.”

A single Delete button on a client record looked simple — until you notice it cascades to every project, invoice, and receipt tied to that client. Those are financial records, not disposable rows. Delete became a soft-delete with a 30-day recovery window instead, and building it surfaced a real bug along the way.

A Bug Found Along the Way

Building the soft-delete migration surfaced a pre-existing bug: a database trigger on the clients table referenced an updated_at column that never existed, meaning every prior "Edit client" save had been silently failing. Fixed in the same migration pass — worth remembering that a feature build is also a chance to find bugs nobody had noticed yet.

30-Day Window

Soft-deleted clients, and everything under them, restore in one click for 30 days, then auto-purge on a nightly scheduled job.

Filtered Everywhere

Every list, dashboard, and report query across the app excludes binned records — swept across roughly twenty queries when this shipped.

Owner/Admin Only

The Bin itself is restricted and kept out of the everyday sidebar groups, so recovering a mistake is deliberate, not a click away by accident.
