Designing for Constraints

Line-Item Views

One document, three levels
of how much detail to show.

Full itemised breakdowns were right for some clients and unnecessarily granular for others — but rebuilding the same quote twice wasn't an option. The fix was a per-document toggle, not a global setting: Itemised, Grouped, and Summary, switchable on the same line items.

Grouped View Iteration

Grouped view first shipped with its own editable "Total shown on PDF" override row — a second source of truth for the total. It was removed once the real Subtotal/GST/Discount/Total box below already covered it; one number, one place it lives. Summary and Grouped overrides also default to the pre-GST amount, not the GST-inclusive total, since that read as confusing tax-inclusive pricing at a glance.

Internal Costing

Purchase price isn't cost —
a subscription bills every year.

Equipment inventory only ever tracked one-time purchase cost and rental rates — useful for quoting gear to a client, useless for knowing what running the studio actually costs per month. A dedicated Internal Costing tab followed, and even that needed one more refinement: a billing-period field, so an annual Adobe subscription converts to the right monthly figure instead of a manually-guessed one.

Billing Period Refinement

Shipped first as a flat "amount → monthly cost" split. That silently assumed everything recurring was billed monthly, which wasn't true — Adobe bills annually. Adding a Billed field (Monthly / Annual) with an auto-calculated, still-editable monthly figure meant the P&L report's overhead number finally matched what the studio was actually paying.

Recurring vs. One-Time

Two costing types, one table: recurring subscriptions and one-time purchases amortised over their useful life, both collapsing to a single "effective monthly cost" column.

Auto-Calculated, Editable

The monthly figure is computed from amount and billing period by default, but stays a plain editable field — the system's best guess, not a locked value.

Feeds the P&L Directly

Active internal costing items sum straight into the P&L report's overhead line, so margin reflects real running cost, not just per-project labor and direct spend.
