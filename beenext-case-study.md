# BEENEXT: Designing a Luxury Distributor Website for Custom Integrators

**Role:** Lead UX/UI Designer
**Year:** 2026
**Deliverables:** User research, brand system, UI design, interactive prototype, content management panel

A short summary before the details: BEENEXT is India's Value Added Distributor for premium AV, IT and automation brands. Their customers are Custom Integrators (CIs), busy professionals who need to trust a distributor before they'll pick up the phone. The goal of this project was simple to state and hard to achieve: make BEENEXT feel like the most credible, premium distribution partner in India, in the first few seconds of landing on the site.

---

## 1. The Brief

BEENEXT's own words framed the challenge best:

> "We needed a website that felt like a premium partner walking into the room, not a product catalogue. Every Custom Integrator visiting should immediately understand this is the best distribution network in India."

That single sentence set the tone for every decision that followed.

### Who we were designing for

**Custom Integrators and System Integrators.** AV and IT professionals who evaluate distribution partners for a living. They are time poor, brand literate, and used to being sold to. Many are checking sites from a phone between site visits, not sitting at a desk with time to read.

### What the business needed

Turn visitors into partner enquiries. The site had to signal premium positioning immediately, build trust through brand authority, and guide Custom Integrators toward the "Become a Partner" path with as little friction as possible.

### What we were working within

A dark, luxury visual language with no shortcuts: gold as the only accent color, sharp architectural layouts, and no rounded corners anywhere. The brief described the feeling as Bang & Olufsen meets enterprise IT. That's a hard needle to thread: warm enough to feel premium, precise enough to feel trustworthy to an engineer.

### The process

Discover, define, design, validate, launch. Research and brand definition came first because everything downstream, the layout, the copy, the tone, depended on understanding what a Custom Integrator actually looks for in those first few seconds.

## 2. Understanding the Custom Integrator

Before touching any visuals, the work started with research: stakeholder interviews with the BEENEXT founders and sales team, an audit of distributor websites across India, Singapore and the UAE, and persona workshops to map out who was actually going to use this site.

The research kept surfacing the same pattern. A Custom Integrator does not read a distributor's website the way a typical visitor reads a marketing site. They scan it, looking for proof, and they decide fast.

**Meet Arjun**, our primary persona. He's an AV Systems Integrator. His goal when he lands on a distributor's site is narrow: confirm they carry the brands he needs before he calls. His frustration is sites that bury the brand list behind a "Contact Us" wall. He scans the hero for brand names, checks the logo strip, and if the brands match what he's looking for, he picks up the phone. If they don't, he leaves.

That behavior led to the most important insight of the whole project:

> Custom Integrators recognize brands before they read copy. Brand identity is their shortcut to trust. Show the logos first, explain everything else second.

### The three questions a CI asks in the first 15 seconds

Research with real integrators kept circling back to the same three questions, so we designed the homepage to answer each one before the visitor has to go looking for it.

| What they're asking | How the site answers |
|---|---|
| "Do they carry the brands I need?" | A brand marquee sits directly below the hero, so every partner brand, Peplink, AVPro Edge, Webro, Murideo, Gude, LILIN, TPI Audio, AVA and SoundLeisure, is visible within one scroll. |
| "Are they a credible, established partner?" | A dedicated stats section puts years in distribution, number of exclusive global brands, and integration partners served in large, confident type. Numbers do the convincing, not adjectives. |
| "How do I get started?" | A "Become a Partner" call to action lives permanently in the navigation, and a full width partner section sits just before the footer, so the path to action is never more than a scroll or a click away. |

Designing for real, specific people rather than an abstract "user" is what made these decisions easy to defend later. Every section either helps Arjun answer one of his three questions, or it earns its place some other clear way.

## 3. Visual Design System

The visual system had to do a lot of work: read as premium without tipping into flashy, and read as technically credible without going cold. Four principles carried that weight.

**No rounded corners.** Every element uses sharp, architectural geometry. It's a small detail, but it consistently reads as precision and confidence rather than the softer, friendlier feeling rounded corners tend to give.

**Gold as the only accent.** Every interactive element, every highlight, every call to action, uses the same gold, `#FFBB50` fading to `#E79D29`. No secondary accent color competes for attention. This keeps the brand instantly recognizable and stops the interface from feeling busy.

**Deliberate weight contrast.** Extra bold headlines set against light body text. That contrast is what gives the typography its editorial, high end feeling, closer to a luxury print publication than a typical SaaS website.

**Dark surfaces throughout.** No white sections anywhere on the site. The luxury feeling has to hold from the first pixel to the last, so the palette stays in black, raisin (`#212121`) and near black surfaces the whole way down.

Typography is Barlow, used as a close, freely licensed match to SF Pro Display, at tight tracking (`-0.03em`) on headlines for that sense of precision, and a light 300 weight on body copy so long paragraphs stay easy to read against the dark background.

## 4. Information Architecture: Every Section Earns Its Place

The homepage follows a deliberate sequence: introduce, validate, demonstrate, convert. Each section is there to answer the question a Custom Integrator is asking at that point in the scroll, not just to fill space.

1. **Hero.** States who BEENEXT is and what they do in one confident line, with the primary and secondary calls to action visible immediately.
2. **Brand Marquee.** Answers "do they carry what I need" before anything else. This section is placed second on purpose, directly under the hero, because making a CI scroll past unrelated content to find brand names would lose them.
3. **What We Do.** Explains the value BEENEXT adds beyond just stocking products: exclusive distribution, a strategy first approach, full stack support, and pan India reach.
4. **Featured Brands.** A closer look at the flagship brands (Peplink, AVPro Edge, Webro, LILIN, Murideo, Gude) in a bento grid, giving the strongest partnerships more visual weight than the rest.
5. **Stats and Why BEENEXT.** This is where credibility gets concrete: years operating, number of exclusive brands, integration partners served. B2B buyers trust numbers over adjectives, so this section is given real space rather than being squeezed into a sidebar.
6. **Solutions.** Shows the verticals BEENEXT serves, corporate AV, residential automation, hospitality, surveillance, pro audio, network infrastructure, so a CI working in a specific niche can quickly see themselves reflected.
7. **Blog Preview.** Signals an active, knowledgeable partner rather than a static catalogue site.
8. **Partner CTA.** Placed deliberately right before the footer. Conversion research consistently shows drop off is highest right at the footer, once a visitor has lost the thread of why they should act. A full width, high contrast call to action re-anchors that value proposition one more time before they leave.
9. **Footer.** Contact details, social links and a clear site map, kept simple because by this point the job of persuading has already been done.

## 5. Designing Inside Real Constraints

Some of the best decisions on this project came from limitations, not inspiration. A few examples worth walking through.

### The hero animation had to earn its place

The hero background uses an animated gold particle grid and hexagonal mesh to bring the dark canvas to life. Early versions piled on far more motion than the message could carry. The headline was competing with the background for attention, and on a page whose first job is to say "trust us" in one confident line, that's a problem. The fix wasn't cutting the motion entirely, it was treating animation like typography: every moving element had to justify why it was there. What survived is the motion that reinforces the brand's energy without pulling focus away from the words. Visitors who prefer calmer motion are also given a quieter version of the same background, so the experience stays comfortable for everyone.

### The brand marquee needed to feel effortless, not heavy

An infinite scrolling strip of nine brand logos sounds simple, but the details matter: it has to move at a pace people can actually read, pause cleanly the moment a CI hovers over a name, and never feel like it's fighting the visitor for control. Getting that pause-on-hover feeling right, calm, immediate, no lag, took several passes. It's the kind of detail a visitor never consciously notices, but they'd notice its absence.

### The stats section needed restraint, not decoration

It would have been easy to over design the numbers section with icons, charts, or extra ornamentation. But the research was clear: what convinces a Custom Integrator is the number itself, large, confident, in gold, with nothing competing for attention. Restraint was the design decision here, and it was the harder one to commit to.

### The content panel had to be usable by someone in a hurry, not a technical person

BEENEXT needed to update hero copy, brand names, stats and blog posts on their own, without waiting on anyone. So the content panel was designed the same way you'd design any other page on the site: a single, calm login screen, then clearly labelled, collapsible sections for hero content, brand marquee, stats, featured brands, blog posts and contact details. Nothing about it tries to be clever. The goal was to make sure whoever is updating a phone number on a Friday afternoon can find the right field and be done in under a minute.

## 6. Structure Before Style

Before any color or type treatment was applied, wireframes tested the layout logic on its own: hierarchy, content density, and where each call to action needed to sit. Every panel was reviewed against the same question: does this match the order a Custom Integrator actually decides in?

- **Hero:** full viewport, persistent navigation with CTA, brand statement above the fold.
- **Brand Marquee + What We Do:** infinite scroll logo strip, followed by four capability cards with a gold top border accent.
- **Featured Brands:** an asymmetric bento grid so the flagship brands get more visual space than the rest, without making the smaller tiles feel like an afterthought.
- **Stats + Why BEENEXT:** three large numbers, three supporting USPs underneath.

Stripping away color at this stage made it much easier to have an honest conversation about whether a section belonged where it was, before any visual polish could distract from that question.

## 7. Outcomes and What I'd Do Differently

### What shipped

A dark, gold accented homepage built around the CI's decision path: recognize the brands, verify the credibility, then act. A calm brand marquee, a restrained stats section, a bento grid for featured brands, and a partner CTA placed to catch visitors right before they'd otherwise leave. Alongside it, a simple content panel so BEENEXT can keep the site current on their own terms.

### What worked well

- Leading with the brand marquee, directly under the hero, consistently tested as the fastest way to earn a CI's trust.
- Keeping the stats section plain and numbers first, rather than decorated, matched how B2B buyers actually evaluate credibility.
- Treating every animated element as something that had to earn its place kept the experience calm and focused on mobile, where most CIs actually encounter the site.
- A CTA in the persistent navigation meant "how do I get started" never required scrolling to find an answer.

### What I'd revisit

- Testing the homepage with a wider group of Custom Integrators before launch, not only with the BEENEXT team, to catch blind spots earlier.
- Sitting with someone at BEENEXT while they used the content panel for the first time, rather than handing it over after it was already finished, to see where they hesitated.
- Spending more time specifically on the mobile experience, since that's where most Custom Integrators are actually meeting this site for the first time.

### The bigger lesson

The strongest design decisions on this project weren't the most visually striking ones. They came from sitting with a real persona's frustration, brands buried behind a "Contact Us" wall, and asking what it would take to remove that friction entirely. Luxury, in this context, wasn't about decoration. It was about respecting a busy professional's time enough to answer their questions before they have to ask.
