Discovery & Brand System

Discovery

Brand principles that drove
every decision.

Principle 01

Premium Restraint

Every element earns its place. Remove what doesn't communicate. The result should feel curated - not busy.

Principle 02

Motion with Purpose

Animation is not decoration. Every transition must convey state, guide attention, or reinforce brand energy. Nothing moves for movement's sake.

Principle 03

Mobile-First Craft

The first visit happens on a phone. Design decisions begin at 390px and scale up - never the reverse.

IA & Conversion Architecture

Section order is as intentional
as section design.

Why Work Is Section #3

Proof before pitch

Showing work before explaining services bypasses the credibility question. The visitor validates quality in their first 8 seconds - then is primed to read how you do it.

Fixed Navigation + Pill CTA

Text links navigate, buttons act

"Start a project" is the only pill button in the nav - all other items are navigation links. Always visible. Nav gains blur background on scroll.

Work Section - Auto-Scroll Marquee

Show more without asking for a click

A horizontal auto-scroll marquee surfaces the entire portfolio passively. Cards are 9:16 - Instagram-native ratio, the same one this audience already scrolls on Instagram.

Primary Conversion Path

Hero → Work → Contact

Average 3 scroll stops from landing to conversion action. Services and Process support credibility - they are not the path.

Designing for Constraints · 01

Design for Constraints

When limitations made
the design better.

The earliest hero leaned hard into the wave reveal and ended up overcrowded - too many moving parts competing for attention in the first three seconds. We audited every motion and asked: does this earn its place?

Animation Audit

The early hero crowded the wave reveal with too many moving parts in the first three seconds. We treated animation like typography: only what communicates energy earns its place. What's left is a single, confident gesture instead of a dozen small distractions.

Designing for Constraints · 02

Testimonials

Light, cream, dark
the third version shipped.

The testimonials section went through three layout passes. A light split-screen felt too corporate for a creative studio. A cream card stack looked warm but visually flat next to the rest of the site. The dark, frosted-glass carousel finally gave it the full-bleed, tactile impact the brand needed.

Testimonials Iterations

Light split, cream stack, dark glass. The first two were dropped on feel, not function - one read too corporate, the other too flat. The frosted-glass version was worth carrying through to the final design.

Designing for Constraints · 03

Case Studies Layout

A masonry grid that got
out of the photography’s way.

An irregular masonry layout competed with the video thumbnails - every card wanted to be the focal point. A uniform 4-column grid with consistent proportions let the work lead. Eight projects, mixed formats, one structure.

Case Studies Layout

Masonry gave each project breathing room but fractured the visual rhythm. A uniform 4-column grid imposed the consistency the content lacked - the constraint of eight diverse projects produced the most disciplined layout on the site.

Ghost Button

Early versions animated the whole ghost button on hover, which felt heavy against the rest of the site's restraint. Now only the arrow moves - a nudge, not a shape flying across the screen.
