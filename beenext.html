Visual Design System

Visual Design System

Dark. Gold. Architectural.

Core Principle

No Rounded Corners

Sharp, architectural geometry reads as precision and confidence - the small detail that consistently separates premium from generic.

Core Principle

Gold as Sole Accent

Every interactive element, highlight and CTA uses the same gold, #FFBB50 fading to #E79D29. No secondary accent competes for attention.

Core Principle

Weight Contrast

Extra bold headlines against light body text create an editorial, high-end feel - closer to luxury print than a typical SaaS site.

Core Principle

Dark Surfaces Only

No white sections anywhere. The palette stays in black, raisin (#212121) and near-black surfaces from the first pixel to the last.

Designing for Constraints · 01

Design for Constraints

When limitations made
the design better.

The hero's animated gold particle grid and hexagonal mesh brought the dark canvas to life - but early versions piled on more motion than the headline could carry. The fix wasn't cutting the motion, it was treating animation like typography: does this earn its place?

Animation Audit

Early motion competed with the words instead of supporting them. We kept only what reinforced the brand's energy, and added a quieter, reduced-motion version of the same background for visitors who prefer calmer motion.

Designing for Constraints · 02

Brand Marquee

Effortless, not heavy
the hover pause took five passes.

An infinite strip of nine brand logos sounds simple. It has to move at a pace people can actually read, pause the instant a CI hovers a name, and never feel like it's fighting the visitor for control - details most visitors never consciously notice, but would notice the absence of.

Brand Marquee Craft

Speed, hover pause, and infinite-loop seams all needed several passes to feel invisible. The version that shipped moves at a readable pace, pauses cleanly on hover with zero lag, and never feels like it's fighting the visitor for control.

Designing for Constraints · 03

Stats Section

The number is the design.
Nothing else competes.

Icons, charts and ornamentation would have been easy to add and easy to justify. But the research was clear: what actually convinces a Custom Integrator is the number itself - large, confident, in gold, with nothing competing for attention. Restraint was the harder decision to commit to.

Stats Section Restraint

Every decorative option - icons, charts, dividers - was tested and cut. The numbers alone, set large and in gold, tested better than any version with added ornamentation.
